01

What does the model learn?

A molecule is a graph: atoms are vertices and bonds are edges. A SMILES string encodes that graph as text. The model maps this structure to properties of the molecule’s electrons.

Energy and spin

Excitation energy is the energy needed to reach an excited electronic state, measured in electronvolts (eV). Singlet and triplet denote total electron spin 0 and 1. The model predicts S₁ and T₁; the evaluator derives their difference.

Absorption strength

The dimensionless oscillator strength f describes the strength of the S₀ → S₁ absorption transition. A low excitation energy does not imply strong absorption. The score includes both a logarithmic transform of f and error on stronger transitions.

02

Evaluation protocol

Each of three prescribed seeds must meet all five validation targets within 60 minutes. Rank by median inclusive elapsed time on the reference allocation.

Preprocessing, compilation, training, validation, and artifact exports count toward the clock. Models train from scratch; external data and pretrained weights are excluded.

Why separate chemical families?

Closely related molecules can make a random split overly optimistic. Here, scaffold and connectivity families remain together. A separate audit contains 31,148 molecules from previously unseen, predominantly rare families; it measures that specific extrapolation, not general performance on arbitrary molecules.

05

Reproduce and contribute

The reference is a 1.90M-parameter bidirectional SMILES transformer. Other structure-based approaches, including fingerprints and graph networks, are welcome.

Submit code, all three runs, predictions, and a frozen checkpoint’s rare-family audit through a pull request. The independent evaluator recomputes scores; maintainers reproduce timing claims.
